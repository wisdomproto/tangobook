## C-37 §1. 앵커 배정

**권**: `who-emptied-the-pool` (c37 · 10쪽 · 4~6세 · 주제군 **C 자연·계절·동물** · 엔진 **오해와 반전** · 무대 콘월 조수웅덩이 · 주인공 어린 게)

**한 줄**: 회청-은빛 냉단색조 워시 필드 + 유일한 빨강 1점(늙은 불가사리) + **물감의 젖음/마름이 조수를 따라간다.** 앵커 슬러그 `changjak-tidepool` — **신규 민팅**.

**이 권이 그림에 요구하는 것(판정의 전제)** 세 가지다. ① **값(value)이 서사다.** 물이 빠졌다(밝음→창백) 돌아온다(창백→밝음). 이 책에서 그림이 해야 할 가장 중요한 일은 「물이 나갔다가 왔다」를 명도·채도의 흔들림만으로 읽히게 하는 것이다. ② **독자가 게보다 먼저 안다.** p2 배경에 물러난 바다가 밝은 은빛 선으로 심기고 p6 에서 그것이 화면 절반으로 회수된다 — 심음과 회수가 **같은 화면 요소의 크기 변화**라서, 그 요소가 매 쪽 같은 물성으로 그려져야 대구가 성립한다. ③ **악센트 1점이 발견 비트다.** 회청·은빛 세상에서 딱 하나의 빨강(늙은 불가사리)이라 p6 에서 시선이 거기 먼저 닿고 곧 멀어진 바다로 흐른다. 대본가가 이미 심어 뒀다(§2.9).

**후보 3.** ① **C6 단색조+악센트 1 · 해양 잉크/과슈 워시(신규 앵커)** — 젖은 워시와 마른 붓이 조수 상태를 그대로 진다. 매체가 곧 서사라 내용-형식 필연성이 최고점이다(§2.3). 리스크는 냉단색조 10쪽이 4~6세에 밋밋해질 수 있다는 것 → p6 한 장에 밀도·빨강·시원한 개방을 몰아 배급해 리듬을 만든다(§2.10). ② **C8 수채 번짐** — 물이 주제라 가장 유혹적이다. 탈락: wet-in-wet 이 화면 전체를 지배하면 **마른 쪽을 그릴 매체가 없다**(조수 대비가 사라진다). 게다가 게가 10쪽에 걸쳐 오해→분개→기다림→환희를 연기해야 하는데 번짐은 인물을 뭉갠다. ③ **C7 회화적 톤**(§7.3 주제군 C 1순위) — 탈락: 예쁜 바닷가 수채를 주지만 **색을 화면 전체에 퍼뜨려 값 규율을 안 세운다**. 물이 빠진 쪽과 찬 쪽이 둘 다 「예쁜 바다 그림」이 되면 이 책의 유일한 사건이 화면에서 사라진다(§7.5 교차관찰 1 — C7 이 가장 평범해지기 쉽다).

**🔴 추천 = 후보 ①.** 주제군 C의 기준선 1순위는 C7 이지만 **엔진이 기준선을 이긴다**(§2.11, A-11 이 주제군 A에서 C3 을 택한 것과 같은 논리). 주제군은 정서를 정하고 엔진은 **그림의 과업**을 정한다. 이 권의 과업은 「값으로 조수를 그리기 + 유일 악센트로 발견 찍기」이고 그건 C6 의 정의다.

**왜 새 앵커인가.** 기존 창작동화 C6 앵커는 A-01(냉회록 안개, 석판 크레용·오일 파스텔)과 A-04(흑연 + 앰버 1점)이고 **둘 다 마른 왁스·흑연 매체**다. 마른 크레용으로 물을 그리면 물이 안 된다. §7.1 이 「앵커 = 팔레트·매체 변주, 앵커당 6~10권」이라 했으므로 **매체가 근본적으로 다르면(마른 왁스 ↔ 젖은 워시) 새 앵커**다. 팔레트도 갈린다(냉회록+주홍 / 회백+앰버 / **해양 은청+올리브+브릭레드**).

**🔴 라인 충돌 확인 (§7.2 분리 규칙).**

## C-37 §2. STYLE ANCHOR

```
STYLE ANCHOR - changjak-tidepool  (young crab / Cornish rock pool / misunderstanding)

Style: a hand-painted picture-book page for 4-6 year olds. Cool, airy, quiet and un-slick.
  The book has one event - the water left and came back - so the PAINT itself has to carry it.

MEDIUM: ink and gouache WASH on cool grey-toned paper with a visible tooth, laid with a soft
  round brush.
  🔴 THE WETNESS OF THE PAINT TRACKS THE TIDE. This is the whole identity of the book:
    - WATER-PRESENT pages are worked WET-IN-WET. Colour bleeds into damp paper, edges melt
      into each other, washes pool and granulate as they dry. The page looks saturated,
      soft-edged and breathing.
    - WATER-GONE pages are worked DRY-BRUSH. The brush skips over the paper tooth, colour
      sits thin and grainy, bare paper shows through the middle of every stroke, edges
      scratch and break off. The page itself looks parched.
  Shapes are made by a wash running out or drying, never by a clean vector line.
  No blending into digital smoothness, no airbrush, no glow.

PALETTE: a cool near-monochrome field dominates every page - silver-grey, wet blue-grey rock,
  and a muted olive-green (seaweed) as a minor recurring note. The cool paper shows through
  everywhere.
  Hex anchors: wet rock #6B7A80 / crevice #45535A / dry mudflat #C7C9C2 /
    water #4E8C8A / bright shallow #7FB6AE / seaweed #5B7A4E / paper #E4E5DF /
    starfish red #C43A2B.
  🔴 EXACTLY ONE warm saturated colour exists in this entire book: a warm brick-red, and it
    belongs to the OLD STARFISH. It appears on ONE page only. On every other page there is
    NO red, no orange, no pink and no warm accent anywhere at all - not on the crab, not on
    the seaweed, not in the sky, not on a shell, not on a pebble. Do not sprinkle warmth
    around for variety.
  🔴 THE NARRATIVE COLOUR IS VALUE, NOT HUE. Water-present = brighter, more saturated
    blue-green, deeper darks. Water-gone = pale, chalky, desaturated silver-grey with the
    contrast squeezed flat. The page must get visibly paler and drier as the water leaves,
    and visibly brighter and wetter as it returns. If the wet pages and the dry pages look
    the same brightness, the book has failed.

MATERIAL TRANSLATION (🔴 keep it painted - never photographic, never plastic):
  - WATER = a translucent wash; the paper tooth shows THROUGH it. Ripples are three or four
    darker brush strokes laid on top. NOT a glassy mirror reflection, NOT an airbrushed
    gradient, NOT liquid-plastic shine, NOT rendered caustics.
  - WET ROCK = flat blue-grey gouache with a darker wash pooled in the crevices, matte.
    NOT a specular highlight, NOT a wet-plastic sheen, NOT rendered stone texture.
  - SILVER MUDFLAT / DAMP SAND = one pale flat wash. The light on it IS the bare paper
    glowing through, not a painted shine. NOT metallic, NOT chrome, NOT a mirror.
  - SEAWEED = a few translucent olive wash strokes that bleed at the tips on wet pages and
    break into dry grain on dry pages. NOT every strand drawn.
  - BUBBLES AND SPRAY = tiny circles of BARE PAPER reserved by painting around them.
    NOT white paint dots, NOT lens sparkles.
  - THE RED STARFISH = the ONE opaque, fully covered matte shape in a world of thin washes.
    It reads as the only solid saturated thing that exists.

COMPOSITION: vast negative space first, subject second.
  The crab is tiny in frame (about 1/7 of page height) on the wide pages and comes forward
  only on the close pages. On the wide pages the receded sea is a single bright silver line
  high in the frame with a huge quiet mudflat beneath it.
  Diagonals lead the eye. Never centre the crab in dead symmetry.
  🔴 MIRROR PAIR: the first pool page and the last pool page use the SAME round pool, the
    SAME slightly-overhead angle and the SAME placement in frame. The only change on the
    last page is that the crab has turned to face the sea (out of frame right) with one
    claw raised in a wave.
  Keep the bottom 18% of the image quiet and free of key subject matter (a caption band is
  laid over it later).

FINISH HIERARCHY - read this twice. This is about how FINISHED each area is, NOT about opacity.
  1. THE CRAB = finished. Wash body, pooled edge, drawn eyes on stalks.
  2. WHAT THE CRAB TOUCHES OR SPEAKS TO on that page (the one rock ledge under it, the one
     periwinkle, the one puddle) = half-finished: contour plus one wash pass.
  3. EVERYTHING ELSE = THE WASH FIELD ITSELF, laid in two or three brush passes and left
     alone. Open shapes, bare paper, nothing described inside it.
  🔴 The empty mudflat is NOT faded, NOT hazy, NOT blurred and NOT out of focus. It is simply
  NOT DRAWN. A confident bare wash is correct; a soft finished haze is wrong.
  🔴 NEVER draw every pebble, every barnacle, every seaweed strand or every ripple. If the
  mudflat is fully described, the single silver line of the far sea stops being visible, and
  the whole book stops working.

CHARACTER DESIGN: eyes are DRAWN on two EYE-STALKS - small dark ovals that can point, cross,
  droop, level off and widen, so the crab can act (eager, indignant, doubtful, joyful).
  NOT dot-eyes on a face. Two claws, eight legs, a round low carapace.
  The crab is a cool shore-crab grey-green so it stays inside the cool world and never
  competes with the red starfish.
  FACE SEPARATION (required): the eye-stalks and their eyes must read apart from the shell in
  VALUE - bare paper or a tonal step where the stalks meet the carapace. Never let the eyes
  sink into one flat body mass. Test: at thumbnail size the mood is still legible.

SETTING: a Cornish rocky shore tide pool - blue-grey rock shelves and ledges, clusters of
  barnacles and periwinkles, strands of green weed, wet sand, a wide low mudflat, and the
  distant sea. British Atlantic coast, cool northern light.
  NO tropical reef, NO coral colour, NO palm, NO warm holiday beach, NO sunset orange.

TIDE-STATE MAP (which paint state each spread is painted in):
  p1  WET   - the pool is full
  p2  DRY   - the shock; the first parched page, and the far sea is planted here
  p3  DRY   - setting out
  p4  DRY   - the periwinkle
  p5  DRY   - the shrimp; drier still
  p6  DRIEST - the revelation. The one red starfish. The far sea fills half the frame
  p7  DRY   - waiting; hot, flat, high noon
  p8  DRY GROUND + ONE WET EDGE - the returning water front slides in across dry sand;
              the transition happens INSIDE the frame, wet-in-wet only along the advancing
              silver edge, dry-brush everywhere ahead of it
  p9  WET   - refilled
  p10 WET   - mirror of p1

CANVAS: 16:9 double-page spread, 4-6 year old picture book.

NOT: NOT digital airbrush / NOT smooth gradients / NOT glossy 3D CG render / NOT cel-shaded
  anime / NOT a paper-texture filter laid over flat digital colour (the wash must MAKE the
  shapes) / NOT photographic / NOT a glassy mirror water reflection / NOT wet-plastic sheen
  on rock / NOT metallic or chrome mudflat / NOT rendered caustics or sparkle highlights /
  NOT a fully rendered background / NOT every pebble, barnacle, ripple or seaweed strand
  drawn / NOT a uniform finish across the page / NOT a hazy, blurry or faded background
  (that is blur, not un-painted) / NOT a second warm accent beyond the one red starfish /
  NOT tropical reef colour / NOT any lettering, numerals, signage or numbers anywhere in the
  image / NOT wool felt, NOT stitched fabric, NOT sculpted clay (other lines own those) /
  NOT dot-eyes on warm cream paper with a red thing on every page (that is another line).
```

## C-37 §3. 캐릭터 시트 (🔴 이것부터 굽는다)

```
CHARACTER SHEET - LittleCrab   (bake this FIRST, before any scene)

🔴 THE SHEET IS PAINTED IN THE SAME MEDIUM AS THE BOOK. Ink and gouache wash on cool
  grey-toned toothed paper #E4E5DF, laid with a soft round brush. The brush grain must be
  visible on the shell, the contour must be left open in places, and a darker wash must be
  pooled along the shell edge where the paint dried. Do NOT render this crab smoothly just
  because there is no background behind it. There is NO red anywhere on this sheet.

FACE: there is no face on the shell. Two EYE-STALKS rise from the top of the carapace, each
  ending in a small dark oval eye #2B2320. 🔴 THE EYE-STALKS ARE THE ENTIRE EXPRESSION
  SYSTEM - the angle of the two stalks is the acting:
    eager      = both stalks tall and tipped forward
    indignant  = stalks pulled in close together and pushed forward like a jab
    puzzled    = stalks splayed, each aimed somewhere different
    waiting    = stalks level, still, both aimed at the horizon
    joyful     = stalks curved outward and lifted
  A tiny mouth line under the front of the shell, with small bubbles of BARE PAPER when happy.
  No dot-eye on the shell, no blush, no glossy catchlight.
BODY: a cool shore-crab grey-green carapace #6F7A66, a slightly warmer underside #8A8B72,
  darker joints #4E5548. Round low shell, wider than tall, painted as ONE flat wash with the
  darker wash pooled along the rim - matte, no plastic shine, no specular highlight, no
  rendered chitin texture.
CLAWS & LEGS: eight thin legs, and two front claws drawn deliberately a little oversized and
  expressive - they point, they droop, they wave. Each leg is one or two brush strokes, not a
  jointed anatomical study.
🔴 NO RED, NO ORANGE, NO PINK ANYWHERE ON THIS CRAB. The red belongs to the starfish alone.
BUILD & SILHOUETTE: a small young crab. Silhouette = round shell + two raised claws + two
  eye-stalks, readable at thumbnail size and instantly distinct from a snail, a shrimp and a
  starfish.
REFERENCE SHEET: full-body from slightly above (the book's main angle) / three-quarter view
  walking sideways with the legs mid-step / front view with both claws raised /
  back view sitting still, seen from behind with both eye-stalks aimed away at a horizon;
  plus FOUR eye-stalk expression close-ups: eager (stalks tall and forward),
  indignant-accusing (one claw pointing, stalks pushed in close),
  doubtful-waiting (stalks level and still, claws lowered and folded),
  joyful (stalks curved, claw raised in a wave, bubbles of bare paper at the mouth).
  Plain cool grey paper background, no scenery, no red.
FACE SEPARATION (required): the eyes and stalks must read apart from the shell in VALUE - bare
  paper or a tonal step where the stalks meet the carapace, never one flat mass. Test: shrink
  this sheet to thumbnail size and the mood must still be legible. This adds no colour.
SCENE token: LittleCrab.
```
